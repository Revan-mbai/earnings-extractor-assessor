PennyMac Mortgage Investment Trust Reports Third Quarter 2026 Results and

Announces Changes to its Leadership Team

WESTLAKE VILLAGE, Calif. – October 29, 2026 – PennyMac Mortgage Investment Trust (NYSE: PMT) today reported net loss attributable to common shareholders of $36 million, or $(0.41) per common share on net investment income of $36 million for the third quarter of 2026. Adjusted net income attributable to common shareholders was $30 million, or $0.35 in adjusted earnings per share (EPS)1.

PMT’s Board of Trustees also approved a $130 million increase to its share repurchase authorization, bringing total available repurchase capacity to over $200 million.

Additionally, PMT today announced that Doug Jones, President and Chief Mortgage Banking Officer, will be retiring on June 30, 2027 after a storied career in the mortgage banking industry including more than 15 years of leadership at Pennymac. His roles and responsibilities will transition to other senior leaders within the company over the next several months.

Also, effective January 1, 2027, Kevin Ryan, currently Chief Strategy Officer, will assume the role of Chief Financial Officer (CFO). Current CFO, Daniel Perotti, will remain in his current role through the remainder of this year and will continue to support the company in an advisory role through the first quarter of 2027. Mr. Ryan will continue to lead the strategy and business development teams while in his new role.

“I am extraordinarily grateful to both Doug and Dan for their long service at Pennymac and their ongoing commitment to and passion for our business,” said Chairman and Chief Executive Officer David Spector. “Both served in various roles and helped to shape Pennymac’s significant growth and transformation into a leading mortgage company. They have made invaluable contributions to the company, and will be greatly missed.”

Mr. Spector continued, “We are very fortunate to have an exceptional leadership team and a deep bench of talent to lead Pennymac into the next chapter, and drive long-term value for our shareholders.”

CEO Commentary

“PMT navigated a challenging third-quarter market backdrop characterized by widening mortgage spreads and sharply rising benchmark interest rates,” said Chairman and CEO David Spector. “While these dynamics pressured the net fair value of our portfolio assets and reduced book value by approximately 5%, our underlying portfolio performed well, generating adjusted EPS of $0.35 per common share, or an annualized adjusted ROE of 9%.1”

Mr. Spector continued, “We have continued to adjust our investment portfolio by rotating capital into organically created, higher-returning credit investments. In recent periods, we have sold or contracted to sell $35 billion in UPB of MSRs, releasing capital from lower-yielding assets and redeploying the proceeds into investments from private-label securitizations which we expect will deliver low-to-mid-teens ROEs. During the third quarter, we completed eight securitizations and retained $167 million in senior and subordinate investments. Additionally, our projected run-rate return potential increased to $0.38 per share. Given these dynamics and considering PMT currently trades at a steep discount to book value, we expect to decrease our current dividend in favor of share repurchases.”

The fair value of PMT’s MSR asset was $3.5 billion at quarter end with $192 billion in UPB of underlying loans, both down slightly from the end of the prior quarter due to previously-announced sales and runoff. The fair value of the MBS portfolio was $4.3 billion, up from $4.1 billion at the end of the prior quarter primarily due to the purchase of $548 million ofAgency floating-rate MBS purchases.

Pretax income for the segment was $36 million, compared to $9 million in the prior quarter and $32 million in the third quarter of 2025. Pretax income in the quarter was driven by $37 million of income excluding market-driven value changes, which was up from $20 million in the prior quarter as a result of lower realization of MSR cash flows due to lower prepayments.

Net loan servicing fees were $242 million, compared to $40 million in the prior quarter. Net loan servicing fees included contractually specified servicing fees and other fees of $144 million, down slightly from the prior quarter, reduced by $85 million in realization of MSR cash flows, which was down from $100 million in the prior quarter due to prepayments. Net loan servicing fees also included $210 million in fair value gains on MSRs, $31 million in hedging losses, and $3 million of MSR recapture income.

Net losses on investments for the segment were $118 million, primarily due to losses on MBS as a result of higher interest rates.

Net interest expense for the segment was $63 million versus $20 million in the prior quarter. Interest income totaled $242 million, up from $237 million in the prior quarter primarily due to increased income from a higher amount of retained investments from private label securitizations. Interest expense was $305 million, up from $257 million in the prior quarter due to higher interest rates and higher financing balances, which includes additional non-recourse asset-backed financing resulting from securitization activity. Both interest income and interest expense were also impacted by market driven value changes in discount accretion due to the significant shift in interest rates during the quarter.

Segment expenses, primarily subservicing fees paid to PFSI, were $25 million, up slightly from the prior quarter.
