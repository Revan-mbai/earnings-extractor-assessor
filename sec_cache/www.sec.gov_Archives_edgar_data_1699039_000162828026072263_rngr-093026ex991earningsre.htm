EXHIBIT 99.1
Ranger Energy Services, Inc. Reports Third Quarter 2026 Financial Results

HOUSTON, TX — (November 9, 2026) — Ranger Energy Services, Inc. (NYSE: RNGR) (“Ranger” or the “Company”) today reported its financial and operational results for the third quarter ended September 30, 2026.
Third Quarter 2026 Financial and Operational Highlights
–Completed the acquisition of STEP Energy Services’ US coil tubing assets on October 8, 2026, creating a leading coil tubing provider in the US, with over $10 million of EBITDA contribution expected in 2027 from pro forma service line
–Secured contracts for four additional ECHO hybrid electric workover rigs with build program running ahead of schedule and positive early customer feedback bringing total fleet in operation or construction to 24 rigs
–Reported Revenue of $162.8 million for the third quarter of 2026 with Net income of $2.5 million, or $0.10 per diluted share and Adjusted EBITDA(1) of $20.1 million, representing an Adjusted EBITDA margin of 12.3%
–Generated Free Cash Flow(2) for the quarter of $20.0 million, fully paying off borrowings, while also completing significant share repurchases during the quarter totaling 142,100 shares, bringing year to date shareholder returns to $11.7M representing 64% of YTD Free Cash Flow

CAPITAL RETURNS UPDATE
During the third quarter of 2026, the Company repurchased 142,100 shares of stock for a total value of $2.2 million, net of tax, at an average price of $15.74 per share. Since the inception of the share repurchase program in 2023 through the end of the third quarter of 2026, the Company has repurchased a total of 4,783,900 shares, for a total value of $54.1 million, net of tax at an average repurchase price of $11.31 per share. PERFORMANCE SUMMARY
Third quarter 2026 revenue was $162.8 million, a decrease of $13.7 million from the second quarter of 2026 and an increase of $33.9 million compared to the third quarter of 2025. The sequential decrease was attributable to lower operating activity across service segments with additional explanation provided in each segment, while the year over year increase primarily reflected the contribution of the AWS business. Cost of services was $138.0 million, or 85% of revenue, in the third quarter of 2026, compared to $142.7 million in the second quarter of 2026 and $109.1 million, or 85% of revenue, in the prior year period. The year over year increase in cost of services primarily reflected the inclusion of AWS in the 2026 results. General and administrative expenses were $7.7 million in the third quarter of 2026, compared to $7.6 million in the second quarter of 2026 and $6.6 million in the third quarter of 2025. Both the second and third quarter of 2026 included additional expenses related to the AWS integration and STEP acquisition.

Net income for the third quarter of 2026 was $2.5 million, compared to $6.9 million in the second quarter of 2026 and $1.2 million in the third quarter of 2025. Fully diluted earnings per share was $0.10 for the third quarter of 2026, compared to $0.29 in the prior quarter and $0.05 in the prior year period.

BUSINESS SEGMENT FINANCIAL RESULTS
High Specification Rigs
In the High Specification Rigs segment, customers have been managing their spend and budgets more closely which moderated activity during the quarter and created incremental white space across the fleet driving down revenue and rig hours. Additionally, as Ranger has gained market share through operator consolidation these past two years, smaller displaced competitors have been offering price reductions in an attempt to regain lost share with some budget-constrained customers opting to capture those potential savings. Ranger has chosen to maintain pricing discipline, and expect it to pay off as the market normalizes and budgets reset next year. Additionally, the ECHO rig program is expected to benefit us in the future as its efficiency gains are solidified pushing down overall service costs for customers.
High Specification Rigs segment revenue was $110.5 million in the third quarter of 2026, a decrease of $2.9 million from $113.4 million in the second quarter of 2026 and an increase of $29.6 million from $80.9 million in the prior year period. Rig hours decreased 2% sequentially to 143,500 from 146,800, and increased 29% year over year from 111,200. Hourly rig rates decreased modestly on a sequential basis to $770 per hour from $772 per hour, and increased 6% year over year from $727 per hour, largely reflecting the pass through of fuel surcharges as well as certain changes in asset and regional revenue mix.
Segment operating income was $8.9 million in the third quarter of 2026, a decrease of $2.3 million, or 21%, from $11.2 million in the prior quarter, and a decrease of $1.1 million, or 11%, from $10.0 million in the prior year period. Adjusted EBITDA(1) was $18.4 million, down from $20.6 million in the second quarter of 2026 and up from $15.7 million in the third quarter of 2025. The sequential decrease primarily reflected lower rig hours and increased costs across categories, but most significantly employee related expenses.
Processing Solutions and Ancillary Services
Within Processing Solutions and Ancillary Services, legacy Rentals and Plugging and Abandonment service lines continued to perform well during the third quarter. Revenue and operating results in certain service lines acquired through AWS, particularly Transportation, Mixing Plants and Tubing Inspection were below our expectations during the quarter, and we are reorganizing these operations to improve focus, utilization and profitability.
Processing Solutions and Ancillary Services segment revenue was $40.0 million in the third quarter of 2026, a decrease of $4.5 million, or 10%, from $44.5 million in the second quarter of 2026, and an increase of $9.2 million, or 30%, from $30.8 million in the prior year period. Wireline Services
The Wireline segment performed as expected this quarter with breaks in customer well programs driving down activity levels. The segment managed this anticipated decline in activity with timely cost reductions, demonstrating the progress made in creating a more responsive cost structure. New work programs began in October, and higher revenue is expected in the fourth quarter along with a return to mid-single-digit margins.
Wireline Services segment revenue was $12.3 million in the third quarter of 2026, a decrease of $6.3 million, or 34%, from $18.6 million in the second quarter of 2026, and a decrease of $4.9 million, or 28%, from $17.2 million in the prior year period. Wireline Completions reported 700 completed stages in the third quarter of 2026, a decrease from 2,560 in the second quarter of 2026 and from 1,800 stages in the third quarter of 2025. The decreases in revenue and completed stages reflect a decline in activity with gaps in customer work schedules.

BALANCE SHEET, CASH FLOW AND LIQUIDITY
As of September 30, 2026, the Company had total liquidity of $76.2 million with no active borrowings on the credit facility. Liquidity consisted of $70.2 million of available capacity under its revolving credit facility and $6.0 million of cash on hand. This compares to total liquidity of $67.7 million as of December 31, 2025, consisting of $57.4 million of available capacity under the revolving credit facility and $10.3 million of cash. The increase in total liquidity was driven by a paydown of the credit facility during the quarter.
Cash provided by Operating Activities was $30.5 million for the quarter and $53.5 million year to date in 2026. Free Cash Flow was $20.0 million for the third quarter and $18.3 million for the first nine months of 2026, compared to $25.8 million in the prior year period. The year over year decrease primarily reflects a growing accounts receivable and contract assets balance resulting from customer payment term changes to 90 days, partially offset by automation efforts to drive down internal receivable processing time.
Cash purchases of property and equipment were $35.2 million for year to date 2026, compared to $19.1 million in the prior year period. Approximately $16.8 million of capital expenditures year to date are related to our ECHO rig program construction payments.

About Ranger Energy Services, Inc.
Ranger is one of the largest providers of high specification mobile rig well services, cased hole wireline services, and ancillary services in the U.S. oil and gas industry. The Company’s services support well operations across the full lifecycle, including completion, production, maintenance, intervention, workover and abandonment phases.

The Company utilizes certain non-GAAP financial measures that management believes to be insightful in understanding the Company’s financial results. Adjusted EBITDA
We believe Adjusted EBITDA is a useful performance measure because it allows for an effective evaluation of our operating performance when compared to our peers, without regard to our financing methods or capital structure. We exclude the items listed below from net income or loss in arriving at Adjusted EBITDA because these amounts can vary substantially within our industry depending upon accounting methods, book values of assets, capital structures and the method by which the assets were acquired. Certain items excluded from Adjusted EBITDA are significant components in understanding and assessing a company’s financial performance, such as a company’s cost of capital and tax structure, as well as the historic costs of depreciable assets, none of which are reflected in Adjusted EBITDA.
We define Adjusted EBITDA as net income or loss before net interest expense, income tax expense, depreciation and amortization, equity‑based compensation, acquisition‑related costs, severance and reorganization costs, gain or loss on sale of assets, significant and unusual legal fees and settlements, impairment of assets, employee retention credit, inventory adjustment, adjustment to contingent consideration, contract liability revenue recognition, and certain other non‑cash and certain other items that we do not view as indicative of our ongoing performance.

Free Cash Flow
We believe Free Cash Flow is an important financial measure for use in evaluating the Company’s financial performance, as it measures our ability to generate additional cash from our business operations. Free Cash Flow should be considered in addition to, rather than as a substitute for, net income as a measure of our performance or net cash provided by operating activities as a measure of our liquidity. Additionally, our definition of Free Cash Flow is limited and does not represent residual cash flows available for discretionary expenditures due to the fact that the measure does not deduct the payments required for debt service and other obligations or payments made for business acquisitions. Therefore, we believe it is important to view Free Cash Flow as supplemental to our entire statement of cash flows.
